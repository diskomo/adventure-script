# Adventure Tours

## 1. What it was

Briefly explain:

* Adventure Tours was a Lua script I ran through Stand in public GTA Online sessions.
* This was before BattlEye made that kind of scripting impractical or impossible.
* It was not a private server, custom game mode, or pre-arranged event.
* It happened inside ordinary public GTA Online lobbies.

Key point to establish:

> Adventure Tours existed inside the normal game, among players who had not asked for it and usually had no idea what it was.

## 2. The world it appeared in

Describe the social environment of GTA Online at the time.

### Legitimate players

Possible themes:

* grinding money;
* repetitive businesses and missions;
* individual progression;
* accumulation of vehicles, properties and status;
* many players sharing a map without necessarily doing anything genuinely communal together.

### Modders

Possible themes:

* selfish enrichment;
* invulnerability;
* griefing;
* trolling;
* kicking or crashing players;
* using asymmetrical power to dominate everyone else.

Then introduce the central contrast:

> I had access to many of the same powers, but I wanted to use them to make the lobby more fun rather than less fun.

**Marxist-adjacent thread:** GTA Online was a densely populated social space whose dominant systems still organised players as isolated competitors pursuing private accumulation.

Avoid making this too grand. Let the contrast speak for itself.

## 3. The yellow bus

Introduce the central object.

Technical/basic information:

* large in-game bus;
* Adventure Tours yellow;
* spawned and controlled through the script;
* capable of being driven, flown or teleported around the map.

More important social function:

* it was visibly absurd;
* it acted as an invitation;
* it gave Adventure Tours a recognisable identity;
* it kept the group together.

Possible line or idea:

> The first version of the idea was basically: what happens if a modder appears next to someone and, instead of exploding them, offers them a seat on a yellow bus?

## 5. The moment strangers became a group

Pause the technical description here.

Explore:

* players began as unrelated people doing unrelated things;
* the bus created a temporary shared purpose;
* passengers became participants rather than isolated players;
* they helped recruit others;
* the identity of the group emerged during the tour.

Possible contrast:

> GTA Online put everyone in the same city. Adventure Tours briefly gave them a reason to move through it together.

**Marxist-adjacent thread:** The important transformation was from co-presence to collectivity: occupying the same world became, temporarily, actually doing something together.

Do not over-explain this. This is one of the central emotional claims of the article.

## 6. How the tours worked

Explain the basic technical structure.

A tour consisted of:

* passenger pickup;
* travel or teleportation to a tour stop;
* spawning enough selected vehicles for everyone;
* applying Adventure Tours colours, modifications and other settings;
* several minutes of activity;
* regrouping;
* moving to the next stop.

The bus was the connective tissue between otherwise unrelated activities.

Possible simple structure:

**Find passengers → Fill the bus → Travel to a stop → Spawn vehicles → Cause chaos for a few minutes → Regroup → Next stop**

Technical details worth mentioning where useful:

* the script handled the tour bus;
* stops were predefined around locations and suitable vehicle sets;
* vehicles could receive selected modifications and special configuration;
* enough vehicles were spawned for the passengers;
* the host controlled the sequence and pacing.

You do not need to list everything.

Important thing to capture:

* the map was being reinterpreted rather than merely consumed as Rockstar intended;
* ordinary or overlooked locations became temporary playgrounds;
* vehicles were selected for what they could produce socially and physically in a group.

**Marxist-adjacent thread:** The existing game world became raw material for collective use rather than a fixed set of officially prescribed, reward-bearing activities.

## 8. Why the stops were short

Explain the pacing philosophy.

Key points:

* activities lasted minutes, not hours;
* no individual activity needed deep progression;
* failure did not matter much because the tour soon moved on;
* chaos was often better than competition;
* variety mattered more than mastery.

Possible point:

> Adventure Tours was not a collection of polished standalone game modes. The tour itself was the game.

Other ideas to explore:

* short activities prevented any one skill level from dominating the experience;
* people could be terrible at something without being excluded from the rest of the night;
* the constant movement created momentum;
* a failed race could still be a successful tour stop.

**Marxist-adjacent thread:** The activities had use-value as shared experiences without needing to become systems of accumulation, ranking or long-term advancement.

## 9. Hosting rather than designing every outcome

Explain your role.

You controlled:

* the route;
* the timing;
* the vehicles;
* the transitions;
* the overall pace;
* the technical machinery.

But the players created:

* jokes;
* rivalries;
* crashes;
* cheating;
* cooperation;
* delays;
* unexpected uses of vehicles;
* the character of each tour.

Key idea:

> The script created the conditions. The passengers created the event.

Possible distinction:

* Adventure Tours was neither fully scripted entertainment nor completely unstructured sandbox play;
* I established a framework, but the social content came from the people inside it.

**Marxist-adjacent thread:** My technical control over the infrastructure mattered, but the actual experience was collectively produced by everyone participating in it.

## 10. The musical horns

Keep this as a small but memorable section.

Technical detail:

* most Adventure Tours vehicles received randomly assigned musical horn notes.

Social effect:

* players quickly discovered that everyone had different notes;
* honking became communal;
* groups produced accidental, usually terrible, horn music;
* it became one of the recurring rituals of the tour.

Possible point:

> It was a tiny scripted feature that became much larger through collective misuse.

The important memory is not merely that the horns were technically different. It is the moment when everyone realised this and began contributing to the noise.

## 11. Why it mattered to me

This is where the political and social reading can become more explicit.

Possible themes:

* GTA Online was full of shared space but often organised around individual accumulation;
* players were encouraged to grind, own, compete and protect;
* modders often reproduced the same selfish logic with greater power;
* Adventure Tours used asymmetrical technical power to create something collective instead.

Possible formulation to expand in your own words:

> The mod menu gave me extraordinary power over the lobby. I could have used that power for myself or against everyone else. Instead, I used it to organise the conditions for strangers to have fun together.

You can connect this to:

* temporary commons;
* non-productive play;
* doing things with no economic reward;
* cooperation without progression incentives;
* the pleasure of shared useless activity.

**Marxist-adjacent thread:** What interested me was not escaping the rules so I could accumulate more than everyone else, but using control over the machinery to suspend the normal competitive logic of the game for a while.

Be careful not to make the passengers into examples in a political essay. Keep the lived experience first and let the analysis arise from it.

## 12. The fact that it was not supposed to exist

This is another major emotional section.

Important elements:

* Adventure Tours was unofficial;
* it appeared inside normal GTA Online;
* players had not opted into a special server;
* the same tools associated with abuse were being used for something communal;
* the intrusion itself was part of the fun.

Possible idea:

> The yellow bus did not wait behind a matchmaking screen. It interrupted the game.

Explore why this mattered.

The original experience depended partly on surprise:

* someone was grinding;
* fighting;
* driving around;
* doing a mission;
* then the bus appeared.

Possible deeper point:

* the contrast between Adventure Tours and ordinary GTA Online gave the project much of its meaning;
* the fact that it emerged through unauthorised scripting made it feel less like consuming another product and more like discovering something players had made inside the cracks of the existing one.

**Marxist-adjacent thread:** There was something satisfying about appropriating a commercial game's infrastructure for a form of social play its official systems neither designed nor particularly rewarded.

## 13. What people said about it

Briefly mention:

* people praised the tours;
* people suggested streaming them;
* people suggested monetising or expanding the idea;
* I knew there might be something larger in it.

Do not let this become a story about “failing to seize an opportunity.”

The important thing is:

> People recognised that it was unusual and worth sharing.

Possible tension to explore:

* other people immediately understood the project through the language available to us: stream it, monetise it, grow it;
* perhaps that was sensible;
* but its value was already real before it became—or failed to become—a product.

**Marxist-adjacent thread:** The fact that something was meaningful did not require it to prove itself by becoming profitable, scalable or professionally legible.

## 14. How it ended

Explain plainly:

* Rockstar introduced BattlEye;
* the technical conditions that allowed Adventure Tours to exist changed;
* I did not stop because I had exhausted the idea;
* the project ended abruptly from the outside.

Possible point:

> It did not feel finished.

Other possible angle:

* Adventure Tours depended on a particular technical opening in GTA Online;
* that opening closed;
* the code was not merely broken by an update—the social environment in which the project could appear was removed.

**Marxist-adjacent thread:** The experience existed on infrastructure I did not control, and when its owner changed the conditions of access, an entire form of player-created activity could simply disappear.

Keep this section restrained.

## 16. What Adventure Tours was

End by defining it in the simplest possible terms.

Possible ideas to work from:

> Adventure Tours was a temporary community formed around a yellow bus.

> It was a way of using tools associated with cheating and domination to create shared play instead.

> It was a group of strangers agreeing, for no practical reason, to stop doing whatever GTA Online expected them to be doing and go somewhere together.

> It was something I made, people joined, and for a while it was real.

Possible final analytical thought:

> In a game built around private accumulation, Adventure Tours created brief pockets of collective abundance: enough vehicles for everyone, no reason to grind for them, and nowhere more important to be than wherever the bus was going next.

Final image to leave the reader with:

* a public GTA Online lobby;
* a yellow bus appearing;
* one person deciding to get in;
* the rest following.

Possible final sentence:

> That was Adventure Tours.
